# Art status and provenance

> **Historical art-pipeline notes.** The paragraph and tables below describe the early procedural-art pass; they are **not an up-to-date statement about assets on main**. As of 2026-11-11, the repository includes owner-supplied painted building/UI sprites, 18 3D hero FBX models with animations, and a Solar Beast Sage sprite-hero prototype. The remaining 10 Elite heroes still lack a finished in-world authored prefab. See `docs/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, `Resources/Config/hero_sprites.json`, and `docs/RELEASE_READINESS.md` for current status and provenance.

**Original historical note (now superseded):** All world art was generated in code; these procedural assets remain as temporary development fallbacks.

## How it is built
| Part | File |
|---|---|
| Mesh tools: curved roofs, pillars, foliage | `Scripts/Game/World/WorldRendering.cs` |
| Buildings, Hero Bases, hero figures | `Scripts/Game/World/BuildingArt.cs` |
| Ground, forest, mountains, water | `Scripts/Game/World/NatureArt.cs` |
| Walls | `WallView` in `Scripts/Game/World/VillageView.cs` |
| Effects and the quality setting | `Scripts/Game/World/WorldEffects.cs` |
| Procedural surface textures | `Scripts/Editor/ArtBaker.cs` |
| Toon, ground and water shaders | `Assets/_Game/Shaders/` |

Art never touches game rules. `BuildingMeshes.For` is the single point to swap in real models, by building id.

## Requirements not fully met
- **Movement not seen:** wind, water flow and lantern flicker are built, but I only reviewed still images. Please check them in Play Mode.
- **Hero idle:** a gentle sway from the shader, not real character animation.
- **Building upgrades:** every building has five texture packs, for levels 1-3, 4-6, 7-9, 10-12 and 13-15: timber and straw, plaster and tile, cut stone, lacquer and iron, marble and gold. Each building keeps its own colour, and higher tiers add posts, trim, banners and a finial. These change materials and dressing, not the base model, so a building does not grow into a new shape. All are generated by code in `BuildingSkins.cs`, with no painted textures.
- **Ambient occlusion:** a cheap darkening near the ground, with no screen-space effect, by design for phones.
- **Phone performance: not measured.** See below.

## Building detail
Every building has its own props and trim in `BuildingDetails.cs`, such as tools, barrels, ropes, drums and rune stones. Roofs have tile colour variation, hip ridges, end tiles and rafter ends. Moss and loose stones fade away as a building is upgraded. The heaviest model, the Dojo, is about 6,200 vertices, and a test caps every model at 7,000.

## Outdoor detail
The mountain has rock cracks, soft ledges, moss patches, shaded snow and rock spires. The forest floor has shrubs, ferns, mushrooms, pebbles, stumps and fallen logs. The pond has lily pads, reeds and stepping stones. Waterfall mist and cloud below the cliff are particles, and those were only built and rendered in stills, not watched moving.

## Measured cost (High quality)
- About 227,000 vertices in view at most, in 17 mesh renderers on 3 materials, with the starter village at level 1. Higher tiers add a little per building. High quality uses 4x anti-aliasing; Low turns it off. Its cost on phones has not been measured.
- Low quality halves plants, stops movement and particles, and turns off shadows. Plant density changes after a restart.
- The world is built when the game starts, and that load time has not been measured on a phone. Baking it ahead of time is the likely fix if it is slow.
- 4K shadow maps are set at High. These may be too heavy for mid-range phones, and Phase 5 should test them.
